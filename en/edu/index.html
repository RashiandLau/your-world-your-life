Before the lesson

  1. Regular play is intended for ages 15 and up; classroom mode is intended for ages 12 and up. Classroom mode fixes the era to the present and removes choices involving alcohol, tobacco, gambling and crime, plus graphic violence.
  2. A life continues through death. Poverty, illness, child mortality, child marriage, conflict and violence may still occur. Preview the experience and offer observation or an alternative activity.
  3. Choose individual play or a teacher-led projected life. On shared devices, avoid student names and school identifiers in notes or the four-digit classroom code.

Sample lesson (25–35 minutes)

  1. Introduce (5 min): explain that the birthplace draw follows birth counts, and individual outcomes are not predictions.
  2. Explore (15–20 min): follow one life. Discuss decisions briefly, then check one statistic and its source year in the final record.
  3. Reflect (5–10 min): choose one approachable worksheet prompt. Students need not connect the story to their own lives.

Common misconceptions

  • Life expectancy is not an age at which everyone dies. A single result follows year-by-year mortality probabilities.
  • One character does not represent everyone in a country. Repeating the same setup can produce different lives.
  • Observed present-day conflict data and future projections are different kinds of evidence. Check dates and estimate labels.
  • Choices can shift probabilities without making poverty or illness a person's fault. Discuss structural conditions alongside individual action.

Care and records

Offer a pause, another activity or a private conversation if someone feels distressed. For immediate danger, use local emergency services and school support procedures. This site is not a support service. Classroom mode temporarily stores the current life in this browser.
