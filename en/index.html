Your World,
Your Life

Your World, Your Life lets you step into one of the lives beginning around the world right now and follow it to the end. About four babies are born each second. You cannot choose your country or family, just as you could not choose your own beginnings.

Classroom mode

This mode uses the present-day world and leaves out choices involving alcohol, tobacco, gambling and crime, as well as graphic violence. Poverty, illness, child mortality, child marriage and conflict may still appear where they relate to the statistics. Your input and results are never sent anywhere.

How to choose a birthplace

The first option weights the draw by annual births in each country; the second weights it by population. Either way, your life begins at age zero.

Length of a life

This is how long it takes to reach that country's average life expectancy. At the 30-minute setting, a year passes in roughly 20–30 seconds. You can pause or speed up time.

You can begin with the default settings.

Serious events such as illness, accidents, poverty and loss can occur at frequencies drawn from statistics.

Born in the same moment

About four other babies are born around the world in the second when your life begins. Their lives follow the same statistics, and you will hear occasional updates.

A moment to pause

Time pauses at ages 5, 15, 30, 50 and 70 for a question. You can think back to that age in your own life. Anything you write is kept with the record.

Which figures are real?

Birth shares, life expectancy, child mortality, income, schooling and urbanisation are approximate public statistics by country. Event probabilities and personality mechanics are models made for this site.

At decisions, you can choose for yourself or let chance decide. From age six, you can change your yearly focus at any time. Choices shift probabilities a little, but your birthplace continues to shape what is possible.

In the past setting, your birth year falls between 1950 and 2025. In the future setting, you are born in 2026 and live through a simplified path based on UN projections.
