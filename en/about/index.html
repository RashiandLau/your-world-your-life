About Your World, Your Life

Your World, Your Life is a free browser-based life simulation. You begin as one of the babies born today and follow a life drawn from public statistics across 172 countries.

What is Your World, Your Life?

Your World, Your Life is a life simulation. You begin in a country and family selected by chance, then follow one person's life from birth to death. Birth shares, life expectancy, income and education figures help shape the path.

There is no score to win. It is a way to reflect on how much of a life begins with chance.

How is my birthplace chosen?

By default, the draw follows each country's share of births across 172 countries. You can instead weight the draw by population. Whether you grow up in a city or rural area, your family's means, and your sex are also selected by chance.

How does a life unfold?

Time moves on its own. From age six, you can choose what to focus on each year. Decisions about school, work, relationships, children and money appear along the way. Your choices change some probabilities, but they do not guarantee outcomes.

At ages 5, 15, 30, 50 and 70, the simulation pauses for reflection and a glimpse of others born at the same moment.

When does a life end?

Each year, the simulation draws from the death rate for that age, country and sex, with a small adjustment for health. A life can end much earlier than average or continue beyond 100.

How long does it take?

Choose about 15 minutes, 30 minutes or one hour on the first screen. That is the time it takes to reach average life expectancy. You can pause or speed up time as you go.

Can I be born in another era?

The past and future settings adjust life expectancy and income using broad historical or projected trends. They are simplified views of those eras.

Where do the figures come from?

Figures draw on the World Bank's World Development Indicators (CC BY 4.0), including WHO/UNICEF DTP3 and measles immunization estimates, the World Happiness Report via Our World in Data (CC BY 4.0), WHO Global Health Estimates and GHO indicators, UNDP expected schooling via Our World in Data, UNAIDS HIV prevalence via Our World in Data, UCDP conflict data via Our World in Data, and UNHCR refugee data. New life-course and governance measures also use World Bank Gender Statistics, ASPIRE, and Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI, CC BY 4.0). Financial and occupational measures also use World Bank Global Findex and ILOSTAT (both CC BY 4.0). Some homicide rates use WHO mortality estimates. Missing values are estimated from comparable countries and marked as estimates. Figures are rounded for this educational site. Many event probabilities are approximations built for this simulation.

Does it cost anything?

No. The site has no paid features.

Do I need an account? Where are my records?

No account is needed. Records stay in this browser (localStorage) and are not sent anywhere. See Help to move them to another device.

Who is it for? Can I use it in class?

The site is intended for people aged 15 and over. Serious events, including illness, accidents, violence and poverty, can occur. The “Reduce distressing scenes” setting softens some descriptions. You may use the site in classes and training.

Can I share a life I've lived?

You can copy a summary from the final record. There is no feature to publish an image or link to a life.

How can I send feedback?

This educational sample has no contact desk. The Feedback button saves a draft in your browser; it does not send a message.
